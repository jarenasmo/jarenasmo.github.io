School Composition and Improvements over Deferred Acceptance

Jorge Arenas

University of Southern California · September 2026

[PDF]

Abstract

The student-optimal stable mechanism (DA), used by most school admission systems, is stable, strategy-proof, and immune to misreports that leave a student’s school unchanged and change her classmates: it is locally non-bossy. We ask whether the mechanisms that correct DA’s inefficiency keep this protection, and conclude that they do not. Conditions on priorities and capacities locate the failure. It needs a chain of denied claims closing at a school with more than one seat, and it is enough that one student has two such chains, each closed while she travels the other. With priorities drawn independently and uniformly, and schools and capacities fixed, that configuration arises with probability approaching one as the market grows. In every problem that has it, Efficiency-Adjusted Deferred Acceptance (EADA) mechanism loses the protection once two identified students waive priorities that could never have helped them.
