Local Non-Bossiness and Improvements over Deferred Acceptance
PDF

In school choice, the student-proposing deferred acceptance mechanism (DA) is stable and strategy-proof, but it may be Pareto inefficient for students. Duque-Rosas, Pereyra, and Torres-Martínez (2026) show that DA is locally non-bossy: a student who changes her report and keeps her school cannot change who else is assigned to that school. We ask whether mechanisms that Pareto improve upon DA inherit this protection. Any improvement over DA preserves the size of every school and the set of unassigned students; hence, when schools have a single seat, every mechanism that improves upon DA is locally non-bossy. With multi-seat schools the conclusion fails: we exhibit an environment in which no Pareto-efficient mechanism that improves upon DA is locally non-bossy. Efficiency adjustments can therefore give a student leverage over her classmates even when they give her none over her school.

NDF and Exchange Rate Volatility (submitted)

with Stephany Griffith-Jones, Catalina Estefó, and Max Gondonneau

PDF

This paper quantifies the impact of non-resident Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) flows on the Chilean peso using daily administrative data. Exploiting the structural break caused by the 2020–2021 pension fund withdrawals, we document a critical regime shift. We find that the retrenchment of local institutional investors eroded market depth, leading to a heightened, nonlinear sensitivity to short-term speculative flows. Post-withdrawal, these offshore positions significantly amplify depreciation and volatility. Our findings underscore the financial stability risks arising when domestic liquidity buffers are compromised, presenting key challenges for monetary policy and regulatory design.

Incentives in Three-Sided Markets

with Juan Pablo Torres-Martínez

PDF

In a class of three-sided matching problems in which the core is non-empty, we show that no stable mechanism is strategy-proof for those who internalize the trilateral structure of the market in their preferences. This impossibility is related to the incompatibility between stability, one-sided strategy-proofness, and one-sided non-bossiness in two-sided markets. Furthermore, unlike what happens in marriage markets, strong restrictions on preferences are needed to ensure that stability and one-sided strategy-proofness are compatible for each market side.

On Incentives in Three-Sided Markets
PDF

In a class of three-sided matching problems that always have stable solutions, we show that no stable mechanism is strategy-proof for those who internalize the trilateral structure in their preferences. Furthermore, strong restrictions on preferences are needed to ensure that stability and one-sided strategy-proofness are compatible for all sides of the market. These results are related to the incompatibility between stability and one-sided group strategy-proofness in two-sided markets.

Reconsidering the Existence of Stable Solutions in Three-Sided Matching Problems with Mixed Preferences

with Juan Pablo Torres-Martínez

Journal of Combinatorial Optimization, 45, Article 62, 2023

Journal PDF

We reexamine the existence of stable solutions in a class of three-sided matching problems previously studied by Zhang and Zhong (2021). The sets of participants are U, V, and W. Agents in U have strict preferences defined on V, agents in V have strict preferences defined on W, and agents in W have strict preferences defined on U × V. In this framework, we show that a weakly stable matching may not exist.
